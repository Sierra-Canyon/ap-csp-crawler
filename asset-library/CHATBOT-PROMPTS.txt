CHATBOT PROMPTS: get a chatbot to make your own art and music
===============================================================

HOW THIS WORKS
    Chatbots are good at TEXT. So you ask for your sprite or song AS TEXT, in the
    format below, and the Asset Workshop (open asset-library/index.html, click
    "Workshop") turns that text into a real .png or .wav file with the right name.

        1. Copy a prompt from this page. Change every part in [SQUARE BRACKETS].
        2. Paste it into the chatbot. Copy the code block it gives back.
        3. Paste that into the Workshop. Look at the preview and the warnings.
        4. Not right? Tell the chatbot what to change (see "FIXING IT" at the bottom).
        5. Happy? Pick what it is for (player, wall, music ...) and press Download.
           Move the file into your  assets  folder. Done.

    A sprite in this format is just rows of characters, one character per pixel,
    which is exactly how your level works. You can edit the pixels yourself in any
    text editor, and you should: it is your sprite.

THE RULES
    * Describe YOUR OWN idea. Do not ask for characters from games, films, shows or
      comics, or for "in the style of" a living artist. Invent something.
    * Follow your teacher's rules about using AI, and say that you used it. A line in
      your README such as "ghost sprite: made with [chatbot name], edited by me" is enough.
    * Check the result. The chatbot can miscount. You are the one who signs it off.

PROMPT 1: ONE SPRITE  (an enemy, a treasure, a weapon, anything that stands still)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
--- COPY BELOW ---
I am making a 16x16 pixel-art sprite for a top-down dungeon game, and I need it as text, not as an image.

Draw: [A ROUND ORANGE PUMPKIN MONSTER WITH A JAGGED GRIN AND A GREEN STEM]
It will be used as: [AN ENEMY]. It is seen from the front, centered, and should fill most of the 16x16 square.

Use exactly this format:

SPRITE [pumpkin] 16x16
PALETTE
. = clear
a = #1a1a1a
(one line per color: a single letter or digit, then = then a hex color)
FRAME 1
(16 rows, each row EXACTLY 16 characters, using only "." and your palette letters)
FRAME 2
(the same sprite with a small change so it looks alive when the two frames alternate, a blink, a bounce, a flicker)
END

Rules:
- "." means a see-through pixel. The background must be ".". Do not fill it in.
- At most 8 colors. Give the shape a dark outline. Put lighter pixels at the top-left and darker at the bottom-right.
- Every row must be exactly 16 characters and every FRAME must have exactly 16 rows. Count them before you answer.
- Reply with ONLY the sprite, inside one code block. No explanation.

Here is a correct example of the format (a green slime), so you can see the layout:

SPRITE slime 16x16
PALETTE
. = clear
a = #142814
b = #a0e66e
c = #6ebe46
d = #fafafa
e = #141414
f = #3c6e28
FRAME 1
................
................
................
................
................
.....aaaaaa.....
....abbbbbba....
...abbcccccca...
..acdecccdceca..
..acccccccccca..
..acccffffccca..
..acccccccccca..
.accccccccccca..
.acfccfccfccfca.
..aaaaaaaaaaaa..
................
END
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    Pointing things (swords, wands, spears): add the sentence
        "It is a weapon lying on its side, pointing to the RIGHT."
    because the game rotates the picture to face the way you swing. Ask for FRAME 1 only.

    The same prompt makes everything the newer tiles need. Change the two [BRACKETS]:
        someone to talk to (N)    "It will be used as: A FRIENDLY CHARACTER WHO STANDS STILL."      save as  npc
        the boss (B)              "It will be used as: THE BOSS. Make it look heavy and dangerous."  save as  boss
        a powerup (*)             "It will be used as: A GLOWING PICKUP." Two frames: bright, dim.   save as  powerup
        an arrow, a stone         add the pointing sentence above. FRAME 1 only.                     save as  arrow  or  stone
        a spell in flight         add the pointing sentence above. Two frames so it flickers.        save as  bolt
    To give ONE spell its own picture, save it as  spell_  and the spell's name:  spell_zap.png
    The boss is drawn a quarter bigger than a tile, so keep its feet on the bottom row.

PROMPT 2: A HERO WHO WALKS  (your player character)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
--- COPY BELOW ---
I am making a 16x16 pixel-art hero for a top-down dungeon game, and I need it as text, not as an image.

The hero: [A YOUNG INVENTOR WITH GOGGLES ON HER FOREHEAD, SHORT BLACK HAIR, A YELLOW COAT AND BROWN BOOTS]

I need six frames. Use exactly these frame names:
  down1, down2    facing the camera (we see the face)
  right1, right2  facing right (side view)
  up1, up2        facing away (we see the back of the head)
Frame 2 of each pair is the same as frame 1 but with one foot lifted, so alternating them looks like walking.
I do NOT need left frames, my tool mirrors the right ones.

Use exactly this format:

SPRITE [inventor] 16x16
PALETTE
. = clear
a = #1a1628
(one line per color: a single letter or digit, then = then a hex color)
FRAME down1
(16 rows of exactly 16 characters)
FRAME down2
...
FRAME right1
...
FRAME right2
...
FRAME up1
...
FRAME up2
...
END

Rules:
- "." means a see-through pixel. The background must be ".".
- At most 8 colors, with a dark outline color. The head is about 8 pixels wide and 7 tall; the body is about 6 rows; the feet are the bottom 3 rows. Keep the hero centered, standing on row 16.
- Keep the head and body in the SAME place in all six frames, so the hero does not jump around.
- Every row must be exactly 16 characters and every FRAME must have exactly 16 rows. Count them before you answer.
- Reply with ONLY the sprite, inside one code block. No explanation.
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    Easier option: open a hero from the library in the Workshop ("Edit as text"),
    paste THAT text into the chatbot, and say:
        "Here is a hero sprite in my text format. Keep the exact format and the same
         shape, but change it into [A PIRATE WITH A GREEN COAT AND AN EYE PATCH].
         Only change palette colors and a few pixels. Reply with only the sprite."
    Changing a sprite that already works goes wrong far less often than starting from nothing.

PROMPT 3: A LOOK FOR YOUR LEVEL  (wall + floor + door that match)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
--- COPY BELOW ---
I am making 16x16 pixel-art TILES for a top-down dungeon game, and I need them as text, not as images.

My level's theme: [A HAUNTED LIBRARY]

Make three separate sprites that look like they belong together:
  1. wall, which blocks the player: [TALL BOOKSHELVES]. It fills the whole 16x16 square, no clear pixels.
  2. floor, which the player walks on: [DARK WOODEN BOARDS]. It fills the whole square, and it must be CALM: low contrast, no big shapes, because it repeats hundreds of times and the hero has to stand out against it.
  3. door, the way to the next level: [A GLOWING TRAPDOOR]. It may use "." for clear pixels; it is drawn on top of the floor.
Tiles repeat side by side, so the left edge must sit comfortably next to the right edge, and the top next to the bottom.

Use exactly this format for EACH of the three, one after another, each in its own code block:

SPRITE [wall] 16x16
PALETTE
. = clear
a = #1a1a1a
(one line per color: a single letter or digit, then = then a hex color)
FRAME 1
(16 rows of exactly 16 characters)
END

Rules:
- At most 6 colors per tile. Share colors between the three so they match.
- Every row must be exactly 16 characters and every FRAME must have exactly 16 rows. Count them before you answer.
- Reply with ONLY the three code blocks. No explanation.
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    In the Workshop, paste ONE block at a time. Save them for your own level as
    wall4.png, floor4.png and door4.png (choose "only on level 4").

PROMPT 4: MUSIC
----------------------------------------------------------------------
--- COPY BELOW ---
Write a short looping chiptune for my dungeon game, as text in the format below (not as audio, not as sheet music).

The mood: [SNEAKING THROUGH A HAUNTED LIBRARY: QUIET, CURIOUS, A LITTLE BIT SPOOKY]

Format:

SONG [haunted library]
TEMPO [a number: slow is 80-100, medium 110-130, fast 140-170]
WAVE [square, pulse, triangle or saw, triangle is soft, square and pulse are classic game sounds, saw is harsh]
LEAD (8 tokens = one bar. Write one LEAD line per bar. Write 8 bars.)
BASS (8 tokens: ONE note per bar, low, octave 2 or 3, the root of that bar's chord)
DRUMS [on or off]

Tokens:
  C4 is middle C. Sharps and flats look like F#4 and Bb3. Octaves 3 to 6 sound best for LEAD.
  -  is a rest (silence for one step).
  _  means "keep holding the previous note for one more step".
  Every token is an eighth note, so 8 tokens make one bar of 4/4.

Rules:
- Every LEAD line must have exactly 8 tokens. Count them.
- Pick one key or scale that suits the mood and stay in it. Use rests. A tune needs room to breathe.
- Make bars 1-4 a phrase and bars 5-8 an answer to it. End bar 8 so that it leads back into bar 1, because the song loops forever.
- Reply with ONLY the song, inside one code block. No explanation.

Example of the format:

SONG town square
TEMPO 126
WAVE pulse
LEAD C5 - E5 G5 - E5 C5 -
LEAD D5 - F5 A5 - F5 D5 -
LEAD E5 - G5 C6 - G5 E5 -
LEAD D5 C5 B4 C5 D5 - G4 -
LEAD C5 - E5 G5 - E5 C5 -
LEAD F5 - A5 C6 - A5 F5 -
LEAD G5 - F5 E5 - D5 C5 B4
LEAD C5 _ _ _ - - - -
BASS C3 D3 E3 G2 C3 F3 G2 C3
DRUMS on
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    Save it as  music  to use it everywhere, or  music4  for your own level only.

PROMPT 5: SOUND EFFECTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
--- COPY BELOW ---
Write very short sound effects for my dungeon game, as text in the format below. Each one is a quick run of notes.

The feel I want: [CUTE AND BOUNCY, LIKE A TOY]

I need these nine, each in its own code block, with these exact names:
  hit (my sword lands), hurt (I get hit), defeat (a monster dies), bump (I walk into a wall), loot (I open a chest),
  potion (I drink a potion), door (I take the stairs down), win (a short fanfare), lose (a short sad ending)

Format for each:

SOUND [hit]
TEMPO [fast: 360 to 600 for most; 180 to 260 for win and lose]
WAVE [square, pulse, triangle or saw]
LEAD [2 to 6 tokens for most sounds; up to 10 for win and lose]

Tokens: notes like C5, F#4, Bb3.  -  is a rest.  _  holds the previous note one step longer.
Going UP sounds happy or rewarding. Going DOWN sounds like damage, falling or failing. One low note is a thud.
Reply with ONLY the nine code blocks.
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    The game has six more sounds. Ask for them the same way, by these exact names:
        shoot (an arrow or stone leaves), cast (a spell), powerup (I pick up a star), talk (someone speaks),
        roar (the boss gets angrier: low and slow), quake (the ground shakes: two low notes, back and forth)

PROMPT 6: IF YOUR CHATBOT CAN MAKE PICTURES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
    Picture-making chatbots do not make real 16x16 pixel art. They make a big picture
    that LOOKS like pixel art. That is fine, the Workshop shrinks it for you
    ("Picture -> sprite" removes the background and makes it 16, 32 or 64 pixels).
    You get the best result by asking for something simple and bold:

--- COPY BELOW ---
Make one image: a single [PUMPKIN MONSTER WITH A JAGGED GRIN], for a retro top-down video game.
Style: chunky pixel art, as if drawn on a 16 by 16 grid, very few, very large square pixels, a thick dark outline, 6 flat colors, no shading gradients, no anti-aliasing, no text.
View: [FROM THE FRONT], centered, filling most of the frame, with nothing else in the picture.
Background: one completely flat, plain [MAGENTA] color that does not appear anywhere in the character.
Square image.
--- COPY ABOVE ---

    Then in the Workshop: Picture -> sprite, choose the image, tick "remove background",
    pick 16 (or 32 if 16 is too blurry), and download. Use "Edit as text" to tidy stray pixels.
    A walking sheet is hard to get this way, use one still picture for the player instead.
    The game flips it when you walk left.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
FIXING IT: what to say when the first try is not right
----------------------------------------------------------------------
    The Workshop tells you exactly what is wrong. Paste its warning back to the chatbot:

        "Frame 'down2' row 7 is 15 wide but should be 16. Fix that row and send the whole sprite again."
        "The character '#' is not in the PALETTE. Add it or replace it."

    To change how it looks, be specific about PIXELS and COLORS, not feelings:

        "Make the outline darker: change a to #101018."
        "The body is too small. Make it 12 pixels wide and 11 tall, centered."
        "Frame 2 looks the same as frame 1. In frame 2 close the eyes and move the whole body down one row."
        "The floor is too busy. Use only 3 colors that are very close together."
        "The tune is too busy. Replace about a third of the notes with  -  rests."
        "Make bars 5 to 8 go higher than bars 1 to 4, then come back down in bar 8."

    Or just fix it yourself: open the text, change a few characters, paste it back in
    the Workshop. Moving one pixel is faster by hand than by asking.
